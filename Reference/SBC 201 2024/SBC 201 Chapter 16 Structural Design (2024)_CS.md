# SBC 201 Chapter 16 Structural Design
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
2024 | SBCNC

> Scope note: This chapter governs **structural design** of buildings and portions regulated by this code (1601.1): methods, load combinations, and minimum loads. Earthquake and wind loads in this chapter are based on **SBC 301**. It is **not** a materials specification (Chs. 18–23 / SBC 303–309) and **not** a dump of live-load tables for SD.

## Regulatory Overview
Assign a **risk category** from Table 1604.5; that category sets importance for earthquake, flood, and wind and can raise seismic design category and nonstructural bracing. Show risk category, seismic importance factor \(I_e\), and wind data on construction documents (1603.1.4–1603.1.5).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Companion loads — SBC 301 (1601, 1602, 1613.1)
Earthquake load effect \(E\) is defined in **SBC 301** §12.4; flood \(F_a\) in **SBC 301** Chapter 5; wind speed \(V\) from Figures 1609.3(1)–(4) or **SBC 301**. Every structure and permanently attached nonstructural component shall be designed for earthquake motions in accordance with **SBC 301** Chapters 11, 12, 13, 15, 17 and 18 as applicable (1613.1); seismic design category may be determined per §1613 or SBC 301. Roof snow load data: **inapplicable to KSA** (1603.1.3).

**SD takeaway:** Put SBC 301 on the SD code sheet with occupancy/risk; do not size members from this chapter.

### 2. Risk category (1604.5, Table 1604.5)
Each building shall be assigned a risk category per Table 1604.5. Where a referenced standard uses an occupancy category, risk category shall not be taken lower. Where a standard would use **SBC 301 Table 1.5-1**, **use Table 1604.5 instead**. Exception: Tsunami Risk Categories III and IV may follow **SBC 301** §6.4.

| Category | Nature of occupancy (Table 1604.5, condensed) |
|----------|-----------------------------------------------|
| **I** | Low hazard to human life: agricultural; certain temporary; minor storage |
| **II** | All others not in I, III, or IV (commentary: typical “standard occupancy”; earthquake and wind importance factors = **1**) |
| **III** | Substantial hazard: primary public assembly OL **> 300**; assembly spaces each OL **> 300** with cumulative **> 2,500**; Group E or I-4 (or combination) OL **> 250**; educational above high school OL **> 500**; Group I-2 Condition 1 with **≥ 50** care recipients; Group I-2 Condition 2 **without** emergency surgery/treatment; Group I-3; any other occupancy OL **> 5,000**; utilities not in IV; toxic/explosive over MAQ posing a public threat |
| **IV** | Essential: Group I-2 Condition 2 **with** emergency surgery/treatment; ambulatory care with emergency surgery/treatment; fire/rescue/ambulance/police and emergency vehicle garages; designated emergency shelters and emergency operations; emergency backup utilities for IV; highly toxic over MAQ posing a public threat; aviation control; critical national defense; water storage/pumps for fire suppression |

Notes: occupant load for this table may use **net** floor area where Table 1004.5 would require gross (Note a). Toxic/explosive III/IV may drop to II if the building official approves a **SBC 301** §1.6.3 hazard assessment showing no public threat (Note b).

**SD takeaway:** Lock risk category with occupancy at SD — hospitals with ER, fire stations, designated shelters, and large assembly/schools are III or IV, not a default II.

### 3. Multiple occupancies and importance (1604.5.1, 1603.1.5)
Two or more occupancies not in the same risk category → assign the **highest**. Structurally separated portions may be classified separately, **unless** a separated portion provides required access, required egress, or shared life-safety components with a higher-category portion — then **both** take the higher. Exception: ICC 500 storm shelter inside a host building keeps the host’s normal occupancy unless the shelter is a designated emergency shelter in Table 1604.5.

Commentary: importance factors used in earthquake and wind **increase** with risk category; Risk Category IV can raise seismic design category and seismic detailing of architectural, mechanical, and electrical components. Construction documents must show \(I_e\) and seismic design category regardless of whether seismic governs the LFRS.

**SD takeaway:** A small clinic or MEP plant that shares egress with a Category IV wing is Category IV; coordinate occupancy and risk with structure before the first massing.
